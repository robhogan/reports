Waiting for bursts of external writes: what an interactive terminal shows

What react/metro#2170 changes for a person watching a Metro build, and what it costs the build, measured on a real PTY, rendered through a terminal model, and benchmarked on Mattermost with the method from the terminal output report.

Thanks to @vzaidman, bursts of outside output now leave far fewer stale status lines, and the erase sequences hold up with wrapping, resizing and on Windows. The status also behaves as well as or better than on main around HMR, key handling and process exit.

Three costs don't appear in the PR's benchmarks, which use piped output and cold builds. All three come from the status redraw path rather than the burst handling, and a 100 ms render limit in the reporter plus restoring the status in log() removes them. Neither change needs a dependency. The patch is under Recommendation.

Bursts and sustained outside output

At 10 lines/s, main leaves a status copy between almost every pair of lines, and the PR leaves one about every 2 s. No line was lost, reordered or mangled in any run, including alternating stdout and stderr bursts.

The status blinks during terminal.log traffic

At 30 lines/s through terminal.log, the status is on screen 52% of the time against 95% on main. log() erases the status and writes the line, but the redraw then waits for the rest of the 33 ms status delay, which is nearly always running during a build. On main, the redraw after a log is usually synchronous. client_log, worker_stdout_chunk and warnings all take this path. If lines land at random within the delay, each costs about 17 ms of status on average, which roughly fits the table (an inference from the code and the measured rates).

Real Mattermost builds agree. At about 3 lines/s from the main thread, the status is on screen 99.7% of the time on main and 95–97% on the PR. With one worker line per module, about 730 lines/s, it's 13–14% and 6–9%. So main blinks too at very high rates, and the fix goes further than restoring it: 98.9–99.8% in those two builds, with a 33 ms limit.

That costs output at high log rates, because the status is written again with each line: 2.29 MB against 0.86 MB for the worker case on main. Cold build time doesn't change, and a warm build with 7,317 lines takes +28 ms against main, compared with +132 ms on the PR.

Build time and main-thread cost

A warm build is 11% slower on the PR, and a limit in the reporter removes the difference. Warm builds are nearly all main-thread work, so the cost of building the status for each of 14,638 progress events reaches build time directly. Cold builds have slack, so build time doesn't move, but the same work adds 328 ms of main-thread CPU. Terminal already limits writes here, so this isn't a write cost.

The PR's own tables measure metro build with piped output. Its wall-time saving comes from no longer waiting on a trailing throttle timer at exit, in non-TTY builds. It's a real gain, separate from the costs here. A piped run of a small fixture exited about 3.3 s sooner than on the base.

Slow readers

Builds on the PR slow down below about 8 KB/s, where main holds to 2 KB/s, because TTY writes block the main thread on POSIX and redraws follow wall time. The earlier report describes that mechanism. A 33 ms render limit doesn't help here, because it's the redraw rate that matters. A 100 ms limit brings output back to about 19 KB and removes the slowdown.

What's unchanged or better

  • No outside output: The bar updates at about 30 Hz against 10 Hz, with no leftovers and the same final state.
  • Wrapping and resizing: Clean at 20 and 40 columns, including multi-line and two-bundle statuses. Narrowing the window from 120 to 40 columns leaves one reflowed line on both sides. The bytes differ (\r against a long cursor-left), and the rendered result is the same.
  • Windows 11 through ssh -tt: Erasing, wrapping, logs and the beforeExit flush render correctly. A burst leaves a copy per line on main and none between its lines on the PR. This ran the Terminal class on its own through conhost, without a full Metro build.
  • HMR: "Done" lines arrive within about 1 ms on both sides, including straight after a console.log. A rebuild under 333 ms following a log shows no bar, which reads as cleaner.
  • Key handling: Fine in raw mode with a menu status. The stale menu copy left on main when a line lands right after a build was gone in the one run of this scenario on each side.
  • Exit: A natural exit writes the latest status. process.exit() and an uncaught error leave the same or a newer status. After Ctrl-C the status stayed on screen in 5 of 6 runs against 6 of 6, with the cursor correct in all of them. The sixth run simply has no status left.
  • Writers bytesWritten can't see: Lines from stdio: 'inherit' children and fs.writeSync(1, …) are erased by the next redraw and a status copy stays, the same on both sides. This is the known limit from react/metro#2130.
  • Separate streams: With a TTY on stdout and stderr piped, stderr bytes count as outside output though they never reach the terminal. That summing already exists on main (60 copies at 10 lines/s, against 3 here), and on the PR the bar updates only about every 2 s in this setup.
  • Piped stdout: 3 status lines over a 6 s build on both sides.
  • API: #isUpdating and #hasPendingUpdate were private, so removing them isn't an API change. [Breaking] is the right scope for the removed event type.

Smaller points

  • Mixed traffic loses the benefit. #write rolls outside bytes into the count, so console.log followed by terminal.log looks quiet to the next status. Alternating the two at 10 lines/s leaves 60 copies on both sides.
  • Old output still delays the next status. The wait compares bytes, not time, so one console.log long ago delays the next status by 333 ms: 336 ms against 1 ms after 3 s of quiet. Log lines aren't affected.
  • Several Terminals at once. 11 or more updating together trigger MaxListenersExceededWarning on beforeExit.
  • Non-TTY waitForUpdates(). With the process kept alive, it takes 3.5 s against 35 ms when the status has changed. Metro, @expo/cli and React Native's community CLI plugin don't call it.

Expo CLI, when it adopts this version

Expo pins its Metro version, so nothing here affects it until then, and none of this needs to hold up the PR. Three things will need attention in @expo/cli at that point.

  • Progress events: MetroTerminalReporter emits bundling:progress from the removed bundle_transform_progressed_throttled event. Moving to bundle_transform_progressed means handling every event, so it needs a limit of its own.
  • Warnings and errors: LogRespectingTerminal clears the status, flushes, writes to stderr directly and restores the status. That write counts as outside output, so each warning hides the status for up to 333 ms. At 10 warnings/s it's on screen 1.7% of a build against 100%. This is one run of the same logic in the harness, not of Expo CLI itself.
  • Types: TerminalReporter.types.ts declares the removed _scheduleUpdateBundleProgress.

Recommendation

  • Limit rendering in TerminalReporter to one progress update per 100 ms. This removes the warm build cost and the slow-reader regression, and it's a timestamp check, so lodash.throttle stays out. The bar stays at the 10 Hz it has today. Without a trailing render, the count on screen can lag until the next event or bundle_build_done.
  • Restore the status in Terminal.log(), in the same write as the line. This removes the blink at every rate measured. It writes more at very high log rates, and if that matters more, the status can be restored only below some rate.
  • Add a test that the status stays on the screen across log(). The new tests use fake timers over a small screen model with real escape handling, which suits this well, and they don't cover it yet.
  • Consider the 333 ms quiet period against lines 333–500 ms apart. Lowering it is a judgement call, and leaving it as it is seems reasonable.
// TerminalReporter.update()

Method

Results are AI-driven. Build measurements follow the terminal output report: a real Metro.runServer serving Mattermost's dev bundle (7,316 modules, 59 MB) to a separate curl process, on a real PTY, with a Python reader at a set rate. Cold means an empty transform cache, warm means all transforms cached. Metro is 0.87.1 with only Terminal.js and TerminalReporter.js replaced at runtime by Babel-compiled versions from the PR's head and base, so the rest of Metro isn't main.

Arms ran in rotating balanced orders. Intervals are bootstrap 95% CIs on the difference in medians, treating runs as independent. The machine is shared: the 1-minute load was about 3–5 during warm runs and 8–14 during cold ones.

To see what a person sees, a recorder drains the PTY as fast as it can and timestamps each read. Each recording is then replayed through @xterm/headless, with a resize applied at its recorded time. The screen is sampled at 60 Hz. "On screen" means a progress line is in the viewport after the latest read, and "bottom line" means it's the last non-empty row. Copies are progress lines in the full scrollback at the end. 120 columns by 40 rows unless stated.

Synthetic scenarios run a 6 s build of 3,000 modules through the real TerminalReporter and Terminal, with progress events every few milliseconds and outside output on a timer. They cover log rates, exit paths, wrapping and HMR-sized rebuilds. Windows runs used a small script around the Terminal class, through ssh -tt to a Windows 11 machine.

Not measured: an emulator painting to a display, so visible flicker is inferred from the terminal state over time. Also real SSH or mobile links, Windows Terminal, a full Metro build on Windows, apps other than Mattermost, and fs.writeSync or stdio: 'inherit' on Windows. The 100 ms limit plus log() fix ran only in the log-rate scenarios and cold builds, so its warm build figure is inferred from the 33 ms version, which renders more often.
